Fundraising

In all the excitement of posting up these pics with the new camera adapter we just got in the mail- I don’t want to forget to mention how great fundraising has been going!  We’re up to $3,471 of our $5,000 goal.  People have been contributing from the road as we’ve been sharing our business cards and blog with everyone who comes up and says:

“HEY- IS IT HOT ENOUGH For yA?!?”

We’ve also met a great collection of people who have worked, lived, eaten, played, biked, hiked and more with individuals affected by Autism Spectrum Disorders.  It’s been really inspiring, everyone loves us for our fundraising goal and the best part is how much we’ve learned along the way.  I wanted to choose a charity that we weren’t familiar with and we didn’t live inside every day in Boston like bicycle advocacy etc.  Well, we’re halfway through the trip and I think that linking up with the Autism-Society was the best decision we made. 

Thanks to all who have donated- I just did a little update on the donation page so if you haven’t already… You can check it out here: http://bit.ly/9lxrRG

August 10th, 2010 4:22pmOriginal post ↗

This is a little place we happened upon called Mr. Ed’s Elephant Museum.  We went down the driveway confused at first because it only looked like the skeleton of what once was an Elephant Museum (whatever that actually is).  When we got closer, we became even more fascinated, a door opened behind us and out pops Mr. Ed himself.  I ask him “What happened here?” to which he replied “We had a fire, two days ago.  We had about 10,000 elephants here (sculptures, toys, etc.) and we were able to save about ¾ths of them.”  Our jaws dropped.  I told Mr. Ed about the house fire that my family experienced when I was but 15 and how I could empathize with the trauma of something like this.  He assured me they already had plans set in place to open a temporary Elephant Museum and (obviously) Candy Shoppe in less than two weeks.  I was so happy to hear that he was feeling resilient and he didn’t let a tragedy like this grind him down.  Even though it was such a bizarre and random place to happen upon. And that we only spoke to him for a few moments- I really had a pretty cool moment with Mr. Ed and will never forget it.

August 24th, 2010 4:31pmOriginal post ↗

After a pretty major structural fail to Greg’s driveside drop out (pictured) we are between a rock(y mountain) and a hard place. During a freak accident during our climb up Monarch Pass (the highest summit of our entire trip at 11,321ft) the mount for the rear dérailleur broke off right through the threads rendering Greg’s bike a singlespeed. This is a pretty major hit but I’m happy to report that morale is high and nothing at all will stop us from making it to San Francisco- we are currently sussing out options to restore somewhere between 8-12 speeds to the bike to make it the rest of the way. In the meantime we are farting around Gunnison, CO while a local shop makes one last ditch effort in putting an emergency der. hanger in place. Stay tuned today while we soak up the beautiful mountain valley weather and wi-fi, and post up all of our favorite pics. from yesterdays climb, bike-fail, and subsequent meltdowns. Thanks to those who have helped us get through from the care package in Parlin to the tips and ideas for shifting solutions they have helped us stay positive and optimistic. If you want to help out- make a donation today to the Autism-Society to let us know you love us and our journey- shifters or not.

August 24th, 2010 4:57pmOriginal post ↗

After hearing me scream from almost 2 miles away- Bianca found me on the side of the road red in the face and teary eye’d over my broken bike and what I thought was the end of the trip. While emotions were running a little too high to Tumblr anything in the heat of the moment- Bianca did capture this gem right after. I had just finished setting the bike up as a single speed by breaking the chain and shortening it and B suggested we take a picture as we puttered up the last ¼ mile of the epic 30 mile climb. I was sure I was going to have to buy a new bike and right as Bianca took this picture she said, “This may be the last time you ride your bike.” It wouldn’t be a trip if nothing went wrong and I’ll remember the feelings running through me when this was shot for the rest of my life.

Finally, when all I had left in me was melted down to a warm goo of failure and dismay- we made it to the summit. I had accomplished my goal of Pueblo to Monarch without chamois bike shorts, and Bianca did the entire pass without using her granny gear! It’s using the little victories to keep you going :)

I’m happy to report that with the generous and miraculously speedy help of Rock N’ Roll Sports in Gunnison, CO the bike has been restored to its former glory and we are back on the road!  With a combination of a few recommendations and ideas a lot better than any of my own- Dave of Rock N Roll drilled through my frame to bolt on a emergency hanger and set me up with a new Ultegra der. and chain!  He even repaired my fender mount (I had been using a Redbull pull-tabs since the middle of Kansas). The bike feels great and I’m forever indebted to these guys.  Onward to Lizard Pass! Hopefully this 10,000+ ft. climb is a little gentler on me and my ride. Thanks for all the support.
