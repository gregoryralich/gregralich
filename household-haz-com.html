02 / The problem

One collection day.
A year of accumulation.

Household hazardous waste days are useful. But a single event can't be the whole solution.

When collection is infrequent, confusing to navigate, or booked to capacity, the leftovers stay put. A shelf in the basement becomes a holding area. Then another shelf. Then another year.

The opportunity: help people make progress between collection days—and make those collection days work better.

03 / How it works

A waste wizard.
Without the maze.

01

Snap it.

Photograph the container and label. Add your town or ZIP code. The service suggests an identification; you confirm the details.

02

Know it.

Build a simple home inventory: what it is, how much remains, its container condition, and where it's stored. Check for damage first. Unknowns stay flagged for review.

03

Move it along.

Get a short, specific path: use or reuse, an approved at-home step, a verified drop-off, or a specialist collection. Include safe interim storage while the item waits.

05 / Moving in

You bought the house.
You inherited the shelf.

Buying a house can mean inheriting the previous owner's paint, cleaners, and mystery containers, too.

There is no consistent household chemical handoff: a simple way to identify the leftovers, understand their condition, and decide what to keep, store differently, or move along.

Make a first inventory while moving in. Keep useful labeled products with their storage guidance, flag unknown or damaged containers for review, and build a local action plan for everything else.

A proposed household chemical handoff: give the next owner an inventory and useful information, instead of an unexplained shelf.

WHAT ARE WE DEALING WITH?

CONFIRM THE LABEL

Leftover latex paint

First distinguish water-based paint from oil-based paint. The next step depends on that answer.

Potential path → DEAL IT YOURSELF

Where local rules allow, fully dried latex paint may go in household trash. Absorbents such as kitty litter can help it dry. This route does not apply to oil-based paint or unknown products.

A real result would show your town's rule, preparation requirements, source, and verification date.

TYPE + CONDITION MATTER

A drawer of batteries

Identify the chemistry and check the condition before selecting a collection option.

Potential path → VERIFIED DROP-OFF

Find a participating retailer or municipal program accepting this battery type. Candidates could include Home Depot or Batteries Plus; acceptance must be checked for the specific branch and item.

Damaged, swollen, leaking, or recalled batteries need a separate specialist route. No blanket “put it in the bin” advice.

NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW

No readable label?

A picture can start the inventory. It cannot establish the contents of an unknown container.

Next path → ASK A SPECIALIST

Flag the item for a qualified local program. Don't open, mix, sniff, or test an unknown substance to identify it.

The service should make uncertainty visible, then route it to someone who can help.

07 / Better storage

A safer shelf.
Starting today.

An inventory isn't just a list of things to get rid of. It's a chance to improve how they're stored right now.

Most waste wizards start with where an item goes. Household Haz Com adds an earlier question: is it safely stored while it's still here?

!

Condition comes first.

Cracked, corroded, bulging, or leaking containers jump to the top of the review queue, regardless of the product category. Active danger takes priority over inventory and disposal planning.

↳

Contain the risk.

For suitable, identified products, reviewed guidance can recommend a compatible, leakproof outer container around the original labeled container. Sorbents such as kitty litter or vermiculite are options only when compatible with the contents and the recommended procedure.

✓

Check the whole shelf.

Help users review shelf stability, heavy containers, incompatible products, access by children and pets, and blocked walkways. Check exposure to heat, ignition sources, and floodwater using product-specific storage requirements.

Damaged containers need prompt attention, not automatic DIY handling. Keep original labels and avoid pouring products into new containers. Corroding containers, unknown leaks, reactive products, and damaged batteries require a specialist route; sorbent and containment instructions cannot be universal.

Better storage can reduce opportunities for leaks, mixing, falling containers, and exposure during floods, fires, earthquakes, or everyday slips and falls. It can't make chemicals safe in every emergency—but it can improve the starting conditions.

08 / Batteries

Make batteries
less baffling.

Practical, friendly help from the kitchen drawer to the right collection point.

Start with familiar questions: What did it power? Is it rechargeable? Can you read the label? Is it intact, swollen, leaking, hot, or recalled? Explain the answer in plain language, then show the next step.

  • Keep or collect?Offer chemistry-specific guidance for everyday storage, terminal protection where appropriate, and keeping loose batteries from shorting against metal.
  • Where, exactly?Match chemistry, size, and condition to a verified collection site. Show accepted items, preparation, limits, fees, and hours before someone makes the trip.
  • Something wrong?Route damaged or recalled batteries separately. Follow manufacturer or local HHW guidance; don't send them to an ordinary retail collection bin. Signs of imminent fire trigger emergency guidance.
  • Keep it moving.Save a simple battery inventory and remind users to complete the handoff. Confirm resolution instead of leaving another pile in the garage.

Storage guidance and battery support are central to the idea. The pilot will test how well they help residents take action.

The operating model / Divert what we can

Save collection day
for what needs it.

Household hazardous waste collection remains the destination for materials without another approved route. Help people resolve the eligible items sooner.

01

Eligible paint.

For confirmed latex or water-based paint, show locally approved drying and disposal guidance. Kitty litter or another approved absorbent can help dry eligible paint; it does not neutralize hazardous chemicals or make oil-based paint, solvents, pesticides, or unknown contents safe for the trash.

02

Battery drop-off.

Sort by actual chemistry and condition, then match each group to a specific accepting location. Home Depot and Batteries Plus are candidate destinations, with different acceptance requirements.

03

HHW collection.

Keep the remaining inventory ready for the local household hazardous waste program. Confirm eligibility, appointments, quantity limits, and packaging instructions with that program.

For intact batteries, keep chemistry groups separate and protect terminals as the receiving program directs. Don't rely on shape alone: an AA-sized battery can have different chemistries. Don't dismantle a battery or device just to sort it.

The intended app would show each store's confirmed lithium and non-lithium rates, charging unit, acceptance conditions, and last-checked date. A single national price would give users false certainty.

In the proposed first phase, Household Haz Com provides inventory and routing support without taking possession of materials. Each receiving provider confirms acceptance and handles accepted materials under its program. Responsibility for any future pickup or collective collection would need to be established with the provider before that service is offered.

09 / The bigger idea

One home has leftovers.
A neighborhood has a pattern.

An inventory becomes more useful when it reveals what a community needs.

See the demand.

With permission, combine material types and approximate quantities across households. Share totals, not addresses or personal inventories.

Coordinate the response.

Help municipalities and qualified service providers plan targeted collection, appointments, or neighborhood pickup when demand makes it practical.

Close the loop.

Record what was reused, dropped off, or collected. Give residents progress they can see and partners demand they can plan around.

Collective coordination means working with qualified collection partners—not asking neighbors to mix, store, or transport each other's chemicals.

10 / The foundation

Start with
what's there.

You can't manage what you haven't identified.

The idea borrows an essential practice from workplace hazard communication: keeping an inventory. OSHA's Hazard Communication standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200, requires a list of hazardous chemicals as part of a written workplace program.

Household Haz Com brings that practical habit home, in language people can use. It's a service inspired by HAZCOM, not a claim that household users must comply with a workplace standard.

11 / A practical first pilot

One place.
A few common problems.

Start with a local web app and a deliberately small scope.

  • Start smallOne municipality or region; paint, household batteries, and common labeled cleaners.
  • Build trustReviewed guidance, confirmed local acceptance, source links, and a last-checked date on every route.
  • Learn quicklyMeasure time to a useful answer, storage improvements, damaged containers routed for review, items resolved, successful drop-offs, and demand for shared collection.
  • Test the modelExplore municipal sponsorship and paid coordination with qualified providers. Keep basic guidance easy to access.
